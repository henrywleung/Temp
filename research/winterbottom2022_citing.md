# Papers citing Winterbottom et al. 2022 (IJCC) and related Ochsner rapid-response papers

Compiled 2026-10-05. **Method limitation:** every citation database and publisher site was blocked by the network proxy. That covered Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Crossref, ResearchGate, PubMed/PMC, Europe PMC, OpenAlex, the IJCC site, AACN, Wiley, Springer/BMC, Ochsner Journal, AHRQ and DuckDuckGo. So no "cited by" list or reference list could be opened directly. Every finding below comes from web-search results and their snippets. Each item has a confidence level:
- **CONFIRMED (snippet)**: a search result explicitly says the paper cites or discusses the Ochsner work.
- **PROBABLE**: the paper appears in searches for the Ochsner work and fits the topic, but no snippet confirms the citation.
- **UNCONFIRMED**: the topic overlaps, but there is no evidence of a citation.

**Bottom line:** the Winterbottom 2022 IJCC paper has very few citations that can be found. The IJCC is a small, new WFCCN journal, and its DOI prefix 10.29173 is poorly indexed. Only one citing paper was confirmed: the editorial in the same issue. The 2021 *Critical Care Nursing Quarterly* paper (Winterbottom & Webre) is the version people cite more. It has 2 confirmed citing works and 1 probable one.

## 1. Target paper: verified citation

**Winterbottom F, Webre H, Gaudet K, Burton J.** A Patient Safety Solution: Evaluation of a 24/7 Nurse-led Proactive Rapid Response Program. *International Journal of Critical Care* 2022;16(2):32-44. Published 2022-09-20. DOI: https://doi.org/10.29173/ijcc32. URL: https://wfccn-ijcc.com/index.php/ijcc/article/view/32. The PDF is titled "A Pre-Post Evaluation of a 24/7 Nurse-led Proactive Rapid Response Program".
- Setting: a 650-bed quaternary academic referral centre (Ochsner Medical Center, New Orleans). Pre/post design, Jan 2014 - Feb 2020. The RRS redesign began in early 2017, and the 24/7 nurse-led proactive RR program launched in Dec 2017.
- Findings: statistically significant decreases in ICU and non-ICU cardiopulmonary arrests, RR consults, unplanned ICU transfers and hospital deaths.

## 3. Papers citing Winterbottom & Webre 2021 (CCNQ)

Verified citation: **Winterbottom FA, Webre H.** Rapid Response System Restructure: Focus on Prevention and Early Intervention. *Crit Care Nurs Q* 2021;44(4):424-430. DOI: 10.1097/CNQ.0000000000000379. PMID 34437321.
- Key findings, 2017-2019: cardiac arrests outside the ICU fell 65%, and ICU arrests fell 27%. ICU admissions from inpatient beds fell 4.7%. The risk-adjusted mortality index fell 27% for patients who had expert proactive rounding encounters. RR nurses were trained in AI, electronic risk-stratification alerts, proactive rounding, teamwork and closed-loop communication.

- **#2 notes:** This AHRQ rapid review (Jan 2018 - Jun 2023 literature; 23 articles) includes the Ochsner restructure as one of the US primary studies on implementing a new RRS. ## 4. "From Reactive to Proactive: A Novel Rapid Response System" (CCN 2025)

Verified citation: **Winterbottom FA.** From Reactive to Proactive: A Novel Rapid Response System. *Critical Care Nurse* 2025;45(2):74-76 (April 2025). PMID 40168007. https://aacnjournals.org/ccnonline/article/45/2/74/32711
- Content: this is a short article in the evidence-based-practice style. It describes the staged restructure into a 24/7 proactive RRS, and a pilot of an automated early warning system "based on a very large database" that gives RRTs about **12 hours of lead time**. It also covers RRN training in AI, risk-stratification alerts and proactive rounding, and discusses continuous vital-sign monitoring, EWS, AI and virtual nursing as replacements for the ICU-nurse consult model.
- **Citing papers found: none.** The article is recent and short.
- **Correction (follow-up check):** search snippets tie the "automated EWS based on a very large database ... 12 hours lead time" sentence to Winterbottom's **AJCC 2025;34(4):317-322 "Rapid Response"** paper. Whether the CCN piece repeats it is unconfirmed. DOI for the CCN piece: 10.4037/ccn2025623. The CCN piece also says the most frequent predictors of deterioration are RR, then HR, SpO2, temperature, SBP and level of consciousness.
- Possible responses or related items in Critical Care Nurse (unconfirmed links): "Integration of Rapid Response Teams and Early Warning Systems to Reduce Cardiac Arrests and ICU Readmissions" 45(4):49 (PMID 40748923); "Using Artificial Intelligence With Rapid Response Teams" 46(1):9; "Reflection on the Use of a Rapid Response Team and the Clinical Effectiveness of Early Warning Systems" 46(1):10.

## 5. "Rapid Response Innovation" (PubMed 42103416)

Verified citation: **Winterbottom FA.** Rapid Response Innovation. *Crit Care Nurs Clin North Am* 2026 (June issue; online 2026-03-10). PMID 42103416. Affiliation: Virtual Critical Care, Ochsner Health. COI: consulting fees, honoraria and travel from Zoll Medical and Baxter Healthcare.
- Content: a review of how RRS are moving from crisis-reactive teams to "intelligent, predictive intervention programs". Topics include cognitive-computing-enhanced detection, **ambient monitoring networks**, dynamic communication architectures and precision-guided response protocols. This is directly relevant to BioButton positioning.
- **Citing papers found: none.** It was published in 2026.

## 6. Kumar et al. 2020 (Ochsner Journal): MEWS

Verified citation: **Kumar A, Ghabra H, Winterbottom F, Townsend M, Boysen P, Nossaman BD.** The Modified Early Warning Score as a Predictive Tool During Unplanned Surgical Intensive Care Unit Admission. *Ochsner J* 2020;20(2):176-181. DOI: 10.31486/toj.19.0057. PMC7310184.
- Findings: 263 consecutive unplanned surgical ICU admissions in 2016. Mortality after escalation was 29.3%. MEWS gave **no clinical benefit** as an early warning tool, because mortality was high at every MEWS level and misclassification was high. This supports the case for better signals than intermittent MEWS.
- Citing papers: none confirmed. Searches turned up other MEWS papers, for example "Role of MEWS in evaluating mortality in postoperative period" (*Asian J Pharm Clin Res*) and a hematologic-malignancy MEWS paper (*Intensive Crit Care Nurs* 2023, S0964339723001039). These are **UNCONFIRMED** as citers. Relevance: HIGH if they cite it.
- Related Ochsner abstract: "The MEWS as a predictor of unanticipated ICU admission at the time of rapid response", *CHEST* 2020 (S0012-3692(20)32789-6). The authors were not verified.

## 7. "Alert to Action" 2023 (Ochsner Journal)

Verified citation: **Fixler A, Oliaro B, Frieden M, Girardo C, Winterbottom FA, et al.** Alert to Action: Implementing Artificial Intelligence-Driven Clinical Decision Support Tools for Sepsis. *Ochsner J* 2023;23(3):222-231. DOI: 10.31486/toj.22.0098. PMID 37711478.
- Findings: Ochsner built a suite of Epic CDS tools driven by a predictive learning algorithm for sepsis. Their alert-to-action ratio was 16.46%, against 8.4-12.1% for all Ochsner BPAs. Across the organisation, BPAs per inpatient day fell and the alert-to-action ratio rose from 8.7% in March to 12.2% in September.
- Citing papers: none confirmed. A search snippet said it is "referenced as a best practice resource in recent sepsis literature", but no specific citing paper was named.
